Teams & follows

Six teams, three players, and a habit that takes five minutes.

The Northern Lights Women's Hockey League is small on purpose: six clubs, a 24-game season, and rinks you can drive to on a weeknight. Here is who plays, who to watch, and how to keep up without letting hockey take over your evenings.

The league at a glance

Founded in 2014 by two rink managers who were tired of driving four hours for a game.

The Northern Lights league started with four clubs playing a twelve-game season in borrowed ice time. Twelve years later it has six, a full schedule, and a waiting list of cities that want in. What has not changed is the scale: the biggest building in the league seats twenty-two hundred people, the smallest seats eight hundred, and on a Friday night in February you can hear a single voice from the far end of the rink.

Most players are paid, most of them part-time, and most of them have another job. There are teachers and nurses in this league, a welder, two graduate students and a pastry chef who is, by every account, the fastest skater in the league. That is part of the appeal: these are people you might stand behind in a grocery line on Monday.

Six clubs
Two in the north corridor, two along the river, two on the harbor side. No road trip in the league is longer than ninety minutes.
A 24-game season
October to March, with four games against each rival. Playoffs are a four-team bracket in the last week of March.
Ticket prices
Nine to sixteen dollars, general admission, sold at the door. Nobody in this league has ever needed a presale code.
One sheet of ice per rink
Which means the zamboni is the most important employee in the building, and the schedule bends around it.

Three players worth following

Not the leading scorers, necessarily. The three we would tell a new fan to watch first, with one thing to look for in each.

NA

Nell Abara

Center, 24 · Cedar Hollow Foxes

Leads the league in assists and in post-whistle conversation. She is not the quickest player on the ice, and that is the point: she slows down a half-step before every pass, which is why her wingers always look open.

Watch for: her first three strides off a face-off. She wins the puck forward and the whole shift changes direction.

JK

Jo Kessler

Goaltender, 29 · Riverside Owls

A former college backup who became the most patient goalie in the league. She plays deep in her crease, stays on her feet, and lets the shooter make the first mistake. Her save percentage has never been the best in the league; her team's record always is.

Watch for: her glove hand, and how rarely she commits to the ice before the shot arrives.

SI

Sasha Ilic

Defense, 21 · Larkspur Union

The youngest player in the league and already running the first power-play unit. She skates the puck out of trouble instead of passing it, which terrifies her coach and delights everyone else in the building.

Watch for: her zone exits. When she gets the puck behind her own net, the next ten seconds are usually worth watching.

How to follow the league without doomscrolling

Four small habits. None of them requires an app, a notification, or a second screen during dinner.

  1. One email. Pick a single club's newsletter. Tuesday mornings, five minutes, done. You will know the injury news, the standings and the one storyline everyone is arguing about.
  2. One podcast. "Between the Pipes" runs about twenty minutes after every Foxes home game: two volunteers, a phone, and an honest argument about the second period. It is the least polished and most useful hockey show we know.
  3. One page. Bookmark the league's Sunday results page and check it once a week. That is the entire routine. You do not need to watch every game to follow a season.
  4. One rule for yourself. Do not follow players you do not actually enjoy watching. There is no prize for being early, and no penalty for missing a Tuesday in January.

Pick a team in five questions

No quiz, no form. Find the sentence that sounds like you and follow the club next to it.

You like a team that makes you nervous
Northside Ravens. Every game is 2-1 and every game is decided in the last four minutes.
You want to watch the best goalie in the league
Riverside Owls. Jo Kessler alone is worth the eleven-dollar ticket.
You are bringing someone who has never seen a game
Cedar Hollow Foxes. Fast, loud, and forgiving of confusion — there is always another goal coming.
You like an underdog with a young roster
Larkspur Union. The youngest team in the league, and the most fun to watch lose a game 5-4.
You want the cheapest ticket and the loudest room
Harbor Point Sirens. Nine dollars at the door and a building that behaves like a playoff game in November.
You enjoy a defensive chess match
Millbrook Cardinals. Two passes, a trap, and forty minutes of patience.

The rivalry you will hear about first

Cedar Hollow and Northside have played for the Lantern Cup since 2016, when a power failure at the Ice Barn left the building lit only by the emergency lights and one brass lantern that a maintenance worker had brought in for the storm. The game finished. Nobody could see the clock. The two clubs have played for a lantern ever since, and the current one is dented from a celebration that got out of hand in 2021.

They meet four times a season, and the November meeting is usually the one that tells you how the winter will go. Cedar Hollow wants to skate; Northside wants to make skating miserable. The games are close because neither team is willing to be the first to change.

There is also the Twin Rinks doubleheader in January, when Riverside and Larkspur play back-to-back on the same sheet of ice with a forty-minute zamboni break in between. It is the best value in the league: two games, one ticket, and a concession stand that runs out of coffee by the second intermission.

Pick a team with a rink you can actually get to on a Tuesday. The game is better in the building than on any screen.

Tess Lindqvist, teams editor

Before you pick a side

  • You do not have to choose a team to enjoy the league. Plenty of our readers just watch good hockey and go home.
  • If you do choose, choose by rink first. A team forty minutes away is a team you will actually see play.
  • Buy the cheapest ticket. In a building this size, there is not a bad seat, only a different view of the same play.

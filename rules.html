Hockey Rules: NHL & PWHL

The NHL is the National Hockey League. The PWHL is the Professional Women’s Hockey League. Both reward speed, skill, and teamwork.

Start with these six ideas

1. Score more goals

A goal counts when the entire puck legally crosses the goal line inside the net. A team normally has five skaters and one goalie on the ice. Penalties and tactical decisions can change that lineup.

2. Three periods

Regulation has three 20-minute periods. The clock stops when play stops, so a game takes longer than an hour. Intermissions give players a break and the rink crew time to resurface the ice.

3. Faceoffs restart play

An official drops the puck between two opposing players. Their teammates line up nearby, ready to gain possession. Faceoffs start periods and restart play after many whistles.

4. Offsides protects the entry

Generally, an attacking player cannot enter the offensive zone ahead of the puck. Watch the blue line: the puck needs to enter before the attacking teammates do. Officials judge entry using skate position.

5. Icing brings play back

Icing generally happens when a team shoots the puck from behind the center red line beyond the opponent’s goal line without a touch. The next faceoff returns to the offending team’s end. Exceptions apply, including when a team is shorthanded.

6. Penalties change the numbers

Tripping, hooking, and other illegal actions can send a player to the penalty box. A minor penalty usually lasts two minutes. The team with more skaters has a power play; the other team is on the penalty kill.

Same foundations, important differences

NHL overtime

In the regular season, a tied game goes to five minutes of sudden-death overtime, normally with three skaters per team. If nobody scores, a shootout decides the winner.

In the playoffs, teams play repeated 20-minute overtime periods, normally five-on-five, until someone scores. There is no shootout.

PWHL overtime

In the regular season, a tied game goes to seven minutes of sudden-death overtime, normally three-on-three, followed by a shootout if needed.

Playoff ties use repeated 20-minute overtime periods, normally five-on-five, rather than a shootout.

The PWHL’s jailbreak rule

In the PWHL, a shorthanded goal can end the scoring team’s minor penalty early. In the NHL, scoring shorthanded does not end your own penalty.

In both leagues, a power-play goal generally ends the opponent’s minor penalty early. Major penalties work differently.

Both leagues allow physical play under their own rules. Not every collision is a penalty, and legal contact is not identical across leagues. Official rulebooks explain the exceptions and are the final authority.

Four words you will hear

  • Shift: One stretch of time a player spends on the ice before changing.
  • Assist: Credit for helping create a goal; up to two players can receive one.
  • Save: A goalie stops a shot that would otherwise enter the net.
  • Pulling the goalie: Replacing the goalie with an extra skater, often while trailing late in a game.

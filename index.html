A beginner's guide to the women's game

Hockey makes sense the moment someone explains it to you.

Rink Ready is a small, independent guide for women who are curious about ice hockey — for the person who has watched a game and quietly wondered what a power play is. You do not need a jersey, a history with the sport, or anyone's permission to start watching.

Why this site exists

Rink Ready started in 2019 as a group text. Marisol Vance had taken her niece to a Cedar Hollow Foxes game and spent most of the third period explaining icing to the woman in the next seat, who had a lot of very good questions. By the drive home there was a shared document; by the next season there was a website nobody had asked for.

We write for the person who is interested and does not want to admit they are confused. Every explainer here is read by someone who has never watched hockey before it goes up, and if they stumble, we rewrite it. Nothing on this site assumes you played, and nothing assumes you grew up in a family that watched.

We are four volunteers, about 1,900 subscribers, and one very patient coach named Bea Halloran who checks our penalty definitions before we publish them.

You don't have to know the rules to enjoy a game. You just have to know where to look.

Marisol Vance, editor

One thing to know before your first game

  • You can sit anywhere, but sit higher up if you can. From the fifth row you can see the shape of the play; from the glass you see the boards.
  • Nobody expects you to follow the puck for sixty minutes. Follow one player for a shift instead, then pick someone else.
  • When the crowd stands up and yells, look at the goal, not at the referee. Nine times out of ten the play is already finished.

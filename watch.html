Where to Watch

On the couch, with friends, or at the rink: choose the way to watch that fits you.

Broadcast rights depend on your country, location, and the specific game. Check the official listing before paying for a service; one subscription does not necessarily include every game.

Find the right broadcast

NHL games

Start with the NHL’s official schedule. Find your matchup, check the listed television or streaming provider, and confirm availability in your area.

National and local games may use different services. An out-of-market package may exclude your local team because of regional blackout rules.

Visit the official NHL schedule

PWHL games

Visit the PWHL’s official website for its schedule and current viewing announcements. The available channel or stream can differ by country and game.

Follow links published by the league or team rather than assuming an unofficial stream is safe or authorized.

Visit the official PWHL website

Hockey close to home

College, minor-league, junior, and community hockey can bring the action closer to you. Look up a nearby team’s official schedule and ticket information.

Compare the total cost, including fees, transportation, and parking. A smaller arena can still deliver a memorable night.

Before you press play

  1. Pick the matchup. Choose a game with a team or player you are curious about.
  2. Check the time zone. The advertised puck-drop time may not be your local time.
  3. Confirm access. Read the provider’s game listing and blackout information before subscribing.
  4. Settle in early. Pregame coverage can introduce players and explain what is at stake.

Your first arena visit

  • Bring a layer. Indoor rinks can feel cool even when it is warm outside.
  • Read the venue policy. Bag limits, ticket rules, and accessibility arrangements vary.
  • Arrive with time to spare. Find your entrance and seat before the opening faceoff.
  • Wait for a stoppage. Follow ushers’ directions when entering your row so you do not block live play.
  • Enjoy it your way. Cheer, listen, or ask a friend what just happened. Nobody starts as an expert.

If the puck feels impossible to follow

Look at the player carrying it and the direction teammates are moving. The camera, players’ sticks, and the crowd’s reaction give you clues. Replays help connect the action to the rules.
